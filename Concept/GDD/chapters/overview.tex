\subsection{Vision \& Design Goals}

\subsubsection*{Design Pillars}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Relentless progression without micromanagement:} The player experiences a clear, tangible increase in power without having to constantly issue commands during combat.
	\item \textbf{Tactical depth through team and build combinations:} Complexity lies in the choice and synergy of heroes, items, and skill trees, not in APM.
	\item \textbf{Every build should be “playably good”:} There are weaker and stronger combinations, but ideally no build dead-ends that feel like traps.
	\item \textbf{Deterministic / controllable crafting instead of pure gear grind:} Progress is predictable; the player works toward concrete goals.
	\item \textbf{Clear loop of focus and reward:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Focus phase: Crafting, team-building, skill allocation, research.
		\item Reward phase: Watching auto-battles, enjoying effects, collecting loot.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Anti-Goals}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{No hard loss of long-term progress:} No systems where the player feels they have “lost everything” after many hours (e.g. permadeath across the entire meta-progression).
	\item \textbf{No action-heavy high-APM gameplay:} Player skill lies in planning and pre-combat decisions, not in reaction time or precise timing during combat.
	\item \textbf{No braindead gear grind:} Purely farming the same content for tiny loot upgrades should be avoided; progress should come from systems like research and deterministic crafting.
	\item \textbf{No pay-to-win mechanics:} Monetization (if present) is limited to cosmetic options.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Target Audience}

\subsubsection*{Primary Audience}

The primary target audience are players with limited time who still want a strong sense of progression and reward:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Casual to midcore players} who have little interest in endless grind.
	\item Players with a \textbf{high need for reward feedback} (endorphin hits from visible progress, new builds, clear power spikes).
	\item Players who enjoy alternating \textbf{concentration phases} (planning, optimizing) with \textbf{relaxation phases} (watching, cashing in loot).
\end{itemize}

In short: \emph{“Not much time, but a craving for strong builds and a satisfying power fantasy.”}

\subsection{Product Positioning \& Reference Titles}

\subsubsection*{References}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Backpack Battles / Teamfight Tactics} (game feel)
	\item \textbf{Path of Exile 2} (depth of progression, skill trees, loot fantasy)
	\item \textbf{Research / battle pass systems} (meta-progression without the monetization component)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Differentiation}

Compared to these references, the game focuses on:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{No active player skill during combat:} In contrast to many ARPGs, the emphasis is not on manually executing skills but on preparing and configuring heroes and builds.
	\item \textbf{Semi-deterministic crafting with small RNG layer instead of loot lottery:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The player can deliberately work toward specific items and effects.
		\item Crafting targets are deterministic, but rolls within a controlled range keep items exciting without derailing progression
		\item Randomness exists but should not be the dominant factor for progression.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Every build should be “viable”:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item There are stronger and weaker setups, but ideally no completely dead options.
		\item The goal is to encourage experimentation instead of punishing it with trap choices.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

% RISK: Deterministic crafting may reduce loot excitement if it becomes too predictable.

\subsubsection*{Business Model}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Buy-to-play:} One-time purchase for the full game.
	\item \textbf{Optional monetization:} Cosmetic-only skins / visual customization for heroes or effects.
	\item \textbf{No-gos:} No pay-to-win mechanics, no loot boxes with gameplay-relevant advantages, no mandatory battle pass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scope \& Constraints}

\subsubsection*{Team \& Resources}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Development:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item 1 lead developer (game design, programming, playtesting).
		\item AI support for coding, testing, and documentation.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Art \& content:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Asset purchases for 3D models, animations, and textures.
		\item Focus on reuse and systemic designs instead of massive one-off content production.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Risks and Constraints}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Ambitious system depth:} Auto-battler, ARPG progression, deterministic crafting, and meta-research together are very demanding for a solo project.
	\item \textbf{Balancing effort:} The claim that “every build should be reasonably strong” makes balancing and content creation significantly more complex.
	\item \textbf{Art and UX effort:} Third-person top-down with clearly readable auto-battles requires good readability, effects, and UI communication, even when relying on purchased assets.
\end{itemize}
