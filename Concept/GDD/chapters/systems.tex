\subsection{System Responsibilities}

This subsection briefly defines what each system is responsible for and what it explicitly does \emph{not} handle. 
Detailed mechanics are described in the dedicated sections (e.g. Skill System, Items and Inventory, Crafting System).

\subsubsection*{Research System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Research System converts long-term play (XP, kills, completed Maps) into unlocks that expand the game:
new Hideout elements, Heroes, Item tiers, Crafting options and higher Map difficulties. It controls the pacing 
at which complexity and power are introduced.

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} grant temporary combat buffs for a single run or Map.
	\item Does \emph{not} decide loot quality directly (it only unlocks higher tiers or new sources).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Skill System \& Crown System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Skill System defines each Hero's active abilities and passive effects based on gained XP and allocated points. 
Hero Passives modify base skills (damage, behaviour, synergies) and shape the role of a Hero within a team.

The Crown System provides an additional endgame layer of powerful, rare upgrades that sit on top of the normal 
Skill/Passive progression and offer long-term goals for highly invested players.

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} manage gear or item stats (handled by Items \& Inventory / Crafting).
	\item Does \emph{not} spawn or control enemies (handled by Map \& Combat System).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Inventory System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Inventory System stores and organises all items and currencies the player owns: gear pieces, crafting materials, 
gold and rare currencies. It defines capacity rules (if any), sorting/filtering behaviour and how items are transferred 
between sources (loot, rewards) and sinks (crafting, selling, upgrading).

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} generate items (handled by Loot System and Crafting System).
	\item Does \emph{not} define item stats or balance (defined in the Items and Crafting sections).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Crafting System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Crafting System consumes items, recipes and currencies to produce new or upgraded items. 
It covers three main mechanic families:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Recipes Crafting:} Transforming inputs into specific item outcomes.
	\item \textbf{Upgrading:} Improving existing items (tiers, affix strength, implicits).
	\item \textbf{Hardening:} Locking in or protecting certain properties at a cost.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} govern drop chances or enemy rewards (Loot System).
	\item Does \emph{not} track ownership or placement of items (Inventory System).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Map \& Combat System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Map \& Combat System takes a configured team of Heroes (including their skills and gear) and a chosen Map setup,
spawns Waves of enemies and executes auto-battles. It decides:
\begin{itemize}
	\item when a Wave or Map is won or lost,
	\item which combat events trigger loot drops,
	\item how much XP each Hero gains per successful Wave.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} decide what exact items drop (item generation is part of the Loot System).
	\item Does \emph{not} change hero builds or gear (handled in the Hub by Skills/Crafting/Inventory).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Loot System}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Loot System transforms combat events (e.g. enemy deaths, Map completion) into concrete rewards: 
items, gold, rare currencies. It controls rarity distributions, item tier chances and special drops, and 
feeds both the Inventory and Crafting Systems.

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Does \emph{not} execute combat or determine victory/defeat.
	\item Does \emph{not} handle long-term unlocks (handled by Research and Meta Progression).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Meta Progression / Battlepass}

\paragraph{Responsibility}
The Meta Progression / Battlepass System tracks long-term objectives across multiple sessions 
(e.g. completed Maps, Waves, specific challenges) and rewards them along a defined track, primarily 
with cosmetic rewards and light account-wide bonuses.

\paragraph{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Keine Pay-to-Win-Effekte: keine direkten, dominanten Combat-Vorteile an Echtgeld gekoppelt.
	\item Steuert keine Kernprogression der Helden (XP, Skills) oder der Items (Tiers, Affixe).
\end{itemize}

\section{Research System}
\label{subsec:research-system}

\subsection{Overview}

The Research System provides long-term, account-wide progression by converting play (XP, kills, completed Maps) into unlocks. 
It controls when new systems, heroes, item tiers and world variants become available and thus paces both power growth and complexity.

Research is organised into multiple \emph{research trees}. For the MVP, three main trees are planned:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Crafting Tree} -- unlocks and deepens all item- and crafting-related systems.
	\item \textbf{World Tree} -- unlocks Map modifiers and enemy-ecosystem variants.
	\item \textbf{Heroes Tree} -- unlocks new Heroes, skill tiers and the endgame Crown system.
\end{itemize}

Inputs to the Research System:
\begin{itemize}
	\item XP (global or research-specific, derived from playtime and Map completions),
	\item Kill counts (monster-type / tag sensitive),
	\item Completed Maps and Difficulties.
\end{itemize}

Outputs from the Research System:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Unlocks of systems and content (Hideout elements, Heroes, item tiers, crafting options, Map tiers),
	\item Access to higher difficulties and new enemy variants,
	\item Endgame enhancement layers (e.g. Crown sockets).
\end{itemize}

% RISK: Research gates a large fraction of the total content; slow research progression can create a feeling of "being locked out".
% TODO: Define target time-to-unlock per tier in the Balance chapter.

\subsection{Research Trees (MVP Scope)}

For MVP, the Research System is structured into three primary trees: \emph{Crafting}, \emph{World} and \emph{Heroes}. 
Each tree focuses on a specific axis of progression and unlocks both systems and content along that axis.

\paragraph{Crafting Tree}

The Crafting Tree defines how quickly and in which order the player gains access to more advanced item manipulation. 
It starts with basic gear usage and gradually unlocks more powerful and more deterministic crafting tools.

\subsection{Research Progression and Pacing (MVP-Level)}

For MVP, the Research System should:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Introduce \textbf{Crafting basics} and \textbf{basic Map empowerment} early, to make loot and Maps meaningful quickly.
	\item Gate \textbf{World variants} (undead/holy) and \textbf{endgame crafting (Gear III, Infuse III, Over-Tempering)} behind mid- to late-game play.
	\item Unlock \textbf{Hero Packs} and \textbf{Skill tiers} step by step, so that players first get to know their builds, before they Crown-Sockets come in to the game.
\end{itemize}

\section{Inventory System}
\label{sec:inventory-system}

\subsection{Overview}

The Inventory System manages all player-owned resources outside of combat. 
It is split by item type into dedicated sub-inventories, but presented as a single, tabbed UI inside the Hideout.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Per item type a dedicated inventory:} each item family (Parts, Remains, Runes, Gear, Modules) has its own capacity and stack behaviour.
	\item \textbf{Single Inventory Menu:} all sub-inventories are accessible via tabs in one consolidated screen for clarity.
	\item \textbf{Hero Gear Slots:} each Hero has 6 equipment slots that are filled from the Gear inventory:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Head, Chest, Arm, Leg, Boots, Charm.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Hideout-only Access:} inventory management (equipping, moving, crafting usage) is only available in the Hideout, not during Maps.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Inventory Categories:}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \emph{Items} (Gear, Modules, Runes): Individual, building-related objects with identity and decision-making quality.
	\item \emph{Materials} (Parts, Remains): Stackable resources that primarily serve as input for crafting and upgrading.
\end{itemize}

The Inventory System itself does not generate items or currencies and does not determine drop chances. 
It acts as the persistent container and organiser for all loot generated by the Loot System and all results created by the Crafting System.

\subsection{Inventory Structure \& Rules}

\subsubsection*{Global Inventory and Sub-Inventories}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Single player inventory:} currently, each player has exactly one main inventory; there are no separate stash tabs or alternate character inventories.
	\item \textbf{Per-type sub-inventories:} within this main inventory, each item type has its own sub-inventory with:
	\begin{itemize}
		\item its own capacity model (stack limits, number of stacks),
		\item type-specific UI representation (tab, iconography),
		\item type-specific sorting/filtering options (future).
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Hero Equipment:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Heroes do not have separate bags; they only have 6 equipment slots (Head, Chest, Arm, Leg, Boots, Charm).
		\item Equipping and unequipping gear always transfers items between Hero slots and the global Gear inventory.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Access and Restrictions}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Hideout-only:} 
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Inventory management is only available in the Hideout (Hub).
		\item During Maps, the player cannot:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item move items,
			\item change gear,
			\item initiate crafting/recycling actions.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{During Maps:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Items and currencies are collected, but not manipulated.
		\item Gear decisions and build changes are postponed until return to the Hideout.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

% RISK: Strong restriction to Hideout can frustrate players who want to inspect or theorycraft mid-run.
% TODO: Consider a read-only inventory view during Maps for inspection without interaction.

\subsection{Loot Pickup \& Interaction}

\subsubsection*{Drops}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Enemies drop:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Parts,
		\item Remains,
		\item Currencies (primarily Gold).
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Drop presentation:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Loot appears as neutral bags on the ground.
		\item Bags are colour-coded by rarity.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Pickup Behaviour}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Manual Pickup:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The player clicks on loot bags on the screen.
		\item Each pickup triggers visual and audio feedback, plus a small text log at the side summarising what was collected.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Optional „Pick All“:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item After each Wave, an optional „Pick All“ function can collect all remaining loot at once.
		% ASSUMPTION (70\%): This is a convenience option and may be gated by research or settings later.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Currencies:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Currency drops from enemies are collected automatically on pickup (no separate item objects).
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Loot Usefulness \& Filtering}

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{No loot filter:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item There is no explicit loot filter planned.
		\item Design assumption: \emph{every drop is useful} as input into crafting or progression, even if indirectly.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

% RISK: Without a loot filter, visual clutter and cognitive load can become problematic at high kill rates.

\subsection{Economic Role of the Inventory}

The Inventory System itself is intentionally designed as a \emph{comfort container}, not as a primary economic pressure tool:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{No hard space pressure:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item The main friction in the economy is not inventory space, but the availability of currencies and materials (especially Gold and high-tier Remains).
		\item Item stack limits are present but relatively generous, mainly to maintain readability and avoid infinite hoarding without cost.
	\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Economic pressure via costs, not slots:}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Gold acts as the main brake on crafting and Hideout actions.
		\item Rare currencies (Crystals, late-game Remains) gate high-impact upgrades and endgame systems.
	\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Crafting System}
\subsection{Gear Crafting}

\paragraph{Overview}
The Gear Crafting System combines deterministic player-driven modification (Affixes) with random initial base generation (Implicits). 
Each gear item is defined by its \textbf{Gear Type} (Head, Chest, Leg, Foot, Gloves, Amulet) and can contain:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Up to \textbf{N Implicits} (rolled automatically on creation)
	\item Up to \textbf{M Affixes} (player-crafted and upgradable)\\
\end{itemize}

Each mechanic type has its own input/output schema, cost structure, and scaling formula.
All crafting transactions are atomic (no partial resource loss).

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent
\textbf{Design Goal:} Create meaningful player agency through controlled randomness and resource-driven progression.
\vspace{1em}

\section{Perks and Traits}


\subsection{Loot and Rarities}
\subsubsection*{Purpose}
This section specifies the complete loot flow from wave setup to final rewards. It is implementation-agnostic and defines inputs, order of operations, and balancing knobs.

\subsubsection*{Key Terms}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Wave Budget $B$}: Total loot value budget allocated to a wave.
	\item \textbf{Spent Budget $U$}: Accumulated loot value already awarded in the current wave.
	\item \textbf{Rarity $R$}: Item quality tier (e.g., Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, \dots).
	\item \textbf{Base Chance $p_{\text{base}}[R]$}: The configured per–rarity drop chance before any modifiers.
	\item \textbf{Unlucky Multiplier $m_{\text{unlucky}}[R]$}: Pity-protection factor derived from recent fails / hits per rarity.
	\item \textbf{Luck Factor $L$}: Linear luck strength; $L=0$ (no bonus), $L=1$ (+100%), $L=2$ (+200%), etc.
	\item \textbf{Luck Scale $k[R]$}: Optional per–rarity scaling of luck (default $k[R]=1.0$).
	\item \textbf{Budget Multiplier $m_{\text{budget}}(U, \text{lootValue}, B, R)$}: Soft damping when $U$ approaches/exceeds $B$, including rarity floors.
	\item \textbf{Effective Tags}: The union of an enemy’s \emph{baseTags} and \emph{bonusTags}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{0) Wave Setup}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Compute Wave Budget $B$}: Derived from wave composition (ranks, counts), map difficulty and level; add variance; initialize $U=0$.
	\item \textbf{Load and Merge Loot Rules}: For all tags present in the wave, prepare the merged rule set (drop pools, min/max drops, rarity guarantees, secret rules).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{1) On Enemy Kill}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Determine Roll Count}: Based on enemy rank/type (e.g., Elite may trigger more rolls than Normal).
	\item \textbf{Build Effective Tag Set}: $effectiveTags = baseTags \cup bonusTags$ (duplicates ignored for logic; weighting optional).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{2) Per Roll (for this enemy)}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Pick Tag}: Randomly select one tag from $effectiveTags$ (optionally bias bonusTags slightly).
	\item \textbf{Resolve Tag Rule}: Activate the corresponding merged loot rule for this tag (yields a list of loot entries to attempt).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{3) Iterate the Tag’s Drop Entries}
For each \emph{loot entry} (which may be a single chance or a list of chances), evaluate one \emph{rarity block} at a time. The system supports \emph{parallel rarity rolls} so that multiple rarities can succeed in the same slot.

\paragraph*{Factor Order (per rarity $R$):}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{Base Chance}: $p_{\text{base}}[R]$
	\item \textbf{Unlucky (Pity)}: multiply by $m_{\text{unlucky}}[R]$
	\item \textbf{Luck (Linear, No Cap)}: multiply by $(1 + L \cdot k[R])$ \
	\emph{Example:} $p_{\text{eff, preBudget}}[R] = p_{\text{base}}[R] \cdot m_{\text{unlucky}}[R] \cdot (1 + L \cdot k[R])$
	\item \textbf{Budget (Soft Brake + Floors)}: multiply by $m_{\text{budget}}(U, \text{lootValue}, B, R)$ \
	$\rightarrow$ $p_{\text{roll}}[R] = p_{\text{eff, preBudget}}[R] \cdot m_{\text{budget}}(\cdot)$
	\item \textbf{RNG Roll}: If success, \textbf{award one item} of rarity $R$ (this slot can yield multiple items if several rarities succeed).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph*{Post-Roll Accounting (per rarity):}
\begin{itemize}
	\item On success: add the item to results; increase $U$ by its \texttt{lootValue}; call Unlucky.OnDrop$(R)$.
	\item On fail: call Unlucky.OnFail$(R)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{4) Secret Drops (Optional, Per Enemy)}
Evaluate secret/conditional drops (e.g., specific tag combinations). These are additional rolls that can yield extra items. They do not interfere with the regular pity counters unless explicitly specified.

\subsubsection*{5) Wave Finalization}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item \textbf{MinDrops Failsafe}: If total items $<$ configured minimum for the wave, add items from the wave’s mixed pool until the minimum is met. This path increases $U$; whether it resets pity per rarity is a policy toggle (recommended: \emph{does not} reset pity to preserve fairness).
	\item \textbf{Rarity Guarantees}: If a minimum per rarity is required, top up those rarities. This path typically \emph{does not} alter pity and may optionally bypass budget damping depending on economy policy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection*{Design Notes}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Parallel Rarity Rolls}: Each rarity is evaluated independently; \emph{all successes drop}. Lower-tier success does not consume or block higher-tier success.
	\item \textbf{Luck Scope}: Luck affects \emph{quality} (rarity chances) only. Quantity remains governed by entries, rules, and budget. If desired, allow certain luck-driven drops to \emph{ignore} budget damping (economy-sensitive).
	\item \textbf{Budget as Soft Brake}: $m_{\text{budget}}$ smoothly reduces chances when $U$ exceeds $B$; rarity floors prevent near-zero results for higher tiers.
	\item \textbf{Tags as Theme Driver}: Use $effectiveTags$ for both regular and secret rolls; optional small biases can keep bonusTags slightly more impactful without excluding baseTags.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Balancing Knobs}
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Per–rarity base chances} $p_{\text{base}}[R]$
	\item \textbf{Unlucky growth/reset rates} per rarity
	\item \textbf{Luck factor} $L$ (from passive tree, map mods, etc.) and \textbf{luck scales} $k[R]$
	\item \textbf{Budget damping} curve parameters and \textbf{rarity floors}
	\item \textbf{Tag pick weighting} (base vs. bonus) and secret-drop criteria
	\item \textbf{Failsafe policies}: whether MinDrops/Guarantees affect pity or budget
\end{itemize}
